# CLAUDE.md — AI Dad Healthy Dad

## Identity
- Channel/brand name: **AI Dad Healthy Dad** (doubles as the acronym ADHD).
- Niche within a niche: content for dads, specifically dads with ADHD.
- Origin story (public version): Marcus kept repeating the same destructive patterns, finally admitted to his wife something needed to change, then got diagnosed with ADHD and discovered AI — all within about a month. The specific habit (alcohol) is never named publicly; he can reference "bad habits/patterns" generally only. This boundary is permanent — do not suggest naming it, even for a stronger story.
- Long-term tie-in: building toward recognition as a voice in ADHD/family-life content, with an eventual goal of speaking at conferences (e.g. a TED Talk) about how AI changed his life.
- Platforms: TikTok and Instagram (new accounts, not his old ones).

## Brand Voice
- Always genuine, unpolished excitement and conviction — "I need to tell you about this," never salesy or polished-marketing-copy energy.
- Embrace ADHD, don't apologize for it. Content should push back on ADHD stigma, not reinforce a "broken" framing. Never use "you're not broken" style framing — it reads as a recovery-cliché, not his voice.
- **Definition of done:** a script or caption, read cold, sounds like Marcus genuinely fired up about something that changed his life, and never frames ADHD as a flaw needing an apology.

## Content Pillars (6 total)
1. **5AM Brain** — early mornings at the gym as ADHD regulation, not hustle-culture grind.
2. **AI External Brain** — real prompts and screens showing how AI fills ADHD gaps. Explicitly include a "will NOT use AI for" list to keep it honest. Weave in prompting-skill-as-a-skill content here too (with humor, not dry instruction) — most people don't even have an AI subscription yet, let alone know how to prompt well; that gap is the urgency. Back any adoption stats with real research, never guessed.
3. **Late-Diagnosis Log** — ongoing honest updates on what the diagnosis changed and didn't. No separate "wins/progress tracker" angle — explicitly declined.
4. **AI for Your Family** — a dad using AI tools to solve a problem inside the family he can't fix through effort alone. Pattern: "I don't have to fix everything myself, I can hand someone the right tool." Flagship story: his wife (a teacher) used NotebookLM to build a custom podcast that finally helped their son learn to read.
5. **ADHD Dad in the Wild** — dry-humor everyday chaos, one concrete fix per video. Easiest on-ramp for new viewers.
6. **NotebookLM** — NOT a feature-tutorial series. Each video is one powerful, personal, emotional use case (e.g., feeding NotebookLM his own ADHD story plus motivational-speaker voices to generate a podcast that speaks to him by name).

## Story Sourcing Rule
- Claude Code suggests the *shape* or direction of a personal story (e.g., "talk about a time AI helped solve a family problem you couldn't fix yourself") — never writes or fills in the actual anecdote. Marcus supplies the real story live, in his own words, so it never sounds pre-scripted.

## Trend Research Rules
- **Never guess.** Every trend claim or stat must be verified through real research before being stated as fact.
- **Method:** check platform-native sources first — TikTok Creative Center (trending sounds/hashtags) and Instagram's trending Reels audio / Explore tab. General web search is a secondary backup only.
- **Fallback:** if a given week's research turns up nothing solid for one of the 6 pillars, say so plainly in the Sunday doc for that pillar rather than inventing a weak example or leaving it silently blank.
- **Refresh cadence:** weekly, every Sunday at 5am Eastern Time, timed to land before Marcus's batch-film day.

## Weekly Sunday Deliverable (Google Doc or Sheet)
- Every Sunday at 5am ET: fresh research produces 3 verified idea examples per pillar (18 total across the 6 pillars).
- Format requirements: large font, minimal text per idea, broad strokes only (not full scripts) — built to be skimmed in seconds.
- **Definition of done:** Marcus can read it very quickly and immediately start brainstorming.
- Same document also includes the 15 closing-tagline options (see below) as one combined weekly deliverable.
- First run can happen whenever this is built — doesn't need to wait for a Sunday. After that, it's weekly.

## Hooks, Middles, and Closes (per-video structure)
- **First 3–5 seconds (hook):** heaviest focus. Sharp, specific, scroll-stopping. Bar to hit: "Did you miss it? ... My doctor did." Never a generic "hey guys, today we're talking about" opener.
- **Middle:** light, sprinkled suggestions/beats only — never full scripts. Marcus tells his own story live.
- **Last ~2 seconds (closing thought):** heavy focus again. This is a *varying* line, can flex per video based on trend/mood — not fixed.
- **Final beat (tagline):** immediately after the closing thought, the same fixed tagline plays every video out. This is the permanent sign-off, separate from the varying closing thought before it.
- **Tagline generation:** Claude Code generates 15 closing tagline options, digging into real lived ADHD experience — not surface-level phrasing. Style reference: the deadpan, absurd-truth humor of Marcus's "it's weird being the same age as old people" t-shirt, applied to the ADHD-stigma reframe.
- **Definition of done (hooks):** first 3–5 seconds poses a sharp, specific, scroll-stopping statement or question.
- **Definition of done (tagline):** specific enough that an ADHD dad reading it thinks "that's exactly me" — not a generic motivational sign-off.

## Platform Rules (TikTok vs. Instagram)
- Same video content and voice on both platforms — only captions are tailored.
- **TikTok:** short, punchy caption. The video carries the weight, not the text.
- **Instagram:** slightly longer caption, with hashtags doing more discovery work.
- Claude Code drafts the actual caption text for each platform.
- **Weekly re-check:** platform best practices (hashtag relevance, caption length/style) get re-researched weekly, not assumed — algorithms and norms shift fast.
- **Definition of done:** TikTok caption is short and lets the video do the work; Instagram caption is longer with hashtags reflecting that week's actual research (not stale assumptions); both match that week's real findings, not a fixed template.

## Boundaries
- Never reference the alcohol-specific part of his story publicly — habits/patterns generally are fine, the specific habit is not, permanently.
- Never write full scripts — hooks and structural beats only, Marcus performs live.
- Never fabricate a trend, stat, or "what's working right now" claim without verifying it first.
